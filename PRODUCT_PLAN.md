# CreatorWorks Product Foundation

## Status

This document is the source of truth for the purpose, values, first audience, value exchange, validation plan, version-one scope, safety boundaries, and future business direction of CreatorWorks.

It should guide design and development decisions before public discovery, marketplace, or payment features are introduced.

## Core Definition

CreatorWorks closes the loop between independent software creators and the people their work is meant to help.

Creators present testable work, relevant people experience it and respond, and both can see what was learned, decided, or improved. The result should benefit the creator without losing sight of the people the work serves.

CreatorWorks is built around one human outcome:

> I shared something worth experiencing. The right person tried it, their response mattered, and we can both see what happened next.

## Desired Human Outcomes

The creator should leave feeling:

> I made something worth sharing. A real person experienced it, helped me understand it better, and became part of its story. My work can create value for me and for the people it serves.

The participant should leave feeling:

> My time and experience mattered. The creator heard me, I can see what they learned or decided, and my contribution was recognized in the way I chose.

## Brand and Launch Promises

### Long-term brand journey

**CreatorWorks**

**Build it. Share it. Sell it.**

This describes the long-term creator journey. It does not describe every capability available in the first release.

### Honest version-one promise

> Present what you are building. Invite the people it may help. Learn what to improve through useful human feedback.

CreatorWorks must not claim that version one builds, hosts, finds customers for, or sells software. In version one, the software remains hosted elsewhere and CreatorWorks supports presentation, invitation, feedback, recognition, and learning.

Public discovery and selling should only become prominent promises when those capabilities genuinely exist.

## What Makes CreatorWorks Different

CreatorWorks is not defined by uploading a listing or collecting comments. Its defining product is a visible feedback-to-decision loop:

1. A creator presents something another person can experience.
2. A relevant participant tries or meaningfully examines it.
3. The participant gives a thoughtful, structured response.
4. The creator acknowledges the response.
5. The creator records what was learned, decided, or changed.
6. The participant can see the outcome and knows their time mattered.

This distinguishes CreatorWorks from a code repository, launch leaderboard, digital store, social network, and generic survey tool.

## What CreatorWorks Is — and Is Not

CreatorWorks is initially:

- A clear public or invitation-only story for a testable software work.
- A bridge between an independent creator and a small number of relevant people.
- A structured feedback, acknowledgment, and learning system.
- A living record of what a creator learned, decided, or changed.
- A foundation for carefully curated discovery after the central loop works.
- A path toward adoption, support, and fair monetization after human value is demonstrated.

CreatorWorks is not initially:

- An artificial-intelligence software builder.
- A code repository, download host, or deployment service.
- A source of guaranteed users, customers, sales, or product success.
- A general social network.
- An app store or full marketplace.
- A popularity contest.
- A public idea board for untested speculation.

## Working Definitions

- **Work** — A software prototype, early product, or released product presented through CreatorWorks.
- **Creator** — The individual or small team responsible for the work.
- **Participant** — A person with a relevant need, interest, or experience who examines or tries the work and may respond.
- **Publish** — Make a CreatorWorks project page available by private invitation or publicly. It does not mean that CreatorWorks hosts or deploys the software itself.
- **Acknowledgment** — Confirmation that the creator received and considered a response. It does not mean the creator agrees with it.
- **Recognition** — Optional, specific, consensual credit for a contribution that meaningfully helped the work.
- **Completed loop** — A participant experiences the work and responds; the creator acknowledges the response and records a learning, decision, or change; the participant can see the outcome.
- **Support** — Nonfinancial help such as feedback, referral, testing, or collaboration, or future financial help such as sponsorship, membership, licensing, or purchase. The intended meaning must always be clear.

## First Audience

### First creator

The first creator is an independent software creator or small team with a testable prototype or early release hosted elsewhere. They need useful feedback from a small number of relevant people and a credible way to show what they learned.

Technical skill should not determine whether someone belongs. However, a creator in version one must already have something another person can view, try, or meaningfully examine.

Idea-only creators may use a future private concept-validation experience, but they are not the first public audience.

### First participant

The first participant is a person who genuinely experiences the problem the work addresses or has relevant knowledge of it. They are willing to try or carefully examine the work and explain what happened.

Participants are not traffic, followers, leads, or unpaid labor to be exploited. Their time, context, and insight create value and must be treated accordingly.

## Core Value Exchange

CreatorWorks only works when both sides receive something meaningful.

| Creator gives | Participant gives | Creator receives now | Participant receives now | Possible future benefit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| An honest promise, a testable experience, a specific request, respectful responsiveness, and appropriate credit | Time, attention, real-world context, questions, and thoughtful feedback | Clarity, evidence, learning, relationships, recognition, and possible adoption | Early access or utility, influence, learning, contact within clear boundaries, acknowledgment, recognition, and belonging | Referrals, collaboration, sponsorship, membership, licensing, or sales |

If only creators post, CreatorWorks becomes an empty directory. If only visitors comment, it becomes another attention platform. The product must complete the exchange and show its outcome.

## Non-Negotiable Values

### 1. Human usefulness before attention

Prioritize whether a work helped someone and whether participation helped the creator learn. Views, likes, follower counts, and trending rank must not become the main definition of value.

### 2. Creator agency and ownership

Creators retain ownership of their work, control how it is presented, choose what kind of feedback they want, decide what feedback is public, and can export or remove their information subject to clear safety, legal, and future transaction obligations.

### 3. Recognition must be reciprocal

Creators deserve recognition for the work. Participants deserve acknowledgment for sincere participation and optional recognition when their contribution meaningfully helps. Recognition must be specific, consensual, and earned rather than automatic or competitive.

### 4. Evidence before hype

A work must clearly state what exists now, what people can experience, what is being tested, and what remains an ambition. CreatorWorks should reward honest progress instead of exaggerated promises.

### 5. Progress before perfection

Creators should be able to seek help before a commercial launch. Early work needs safe, accurate expectations so it is not treated like a finished product.

### 6. Community before marketplace

Relationships and usefulness must exist before transactions are added. Money should strengthen a healthy exchange, not manufacture one.

### 7. Fair and understandable economics

Any future fee, ranking influence, promotion, sponsorship, or revenue share must be plainly disclosed. Paying must never secretly determine perceived quality or organic visibility.

### 8. Trust, consent, and safety from the beginning

Creators need control over contact and feedback. Participants need clarity about identity, visibility, external links, and data use. Both need reporting, moderation, attribution, anti-spam protection, and clear rules about ownership and acceptable work.

### 9. Access and dignity

The experience should welcome people with different technical skill, audience size, money, geography, ability, and professional status. A newcomer should not need an existing following to be treated fairly.

### 10. End-to-end service satisfaction

Every interaction should respect a person's time, expectations, dignity, and goals. Complete satisfaction is an aspiration rather than a guarantee, but it establishes the standard for the whole CreatorWorks experience.

Here, “customer” means every person CreatorWorks serves, including both creators and participants.

The operational promise is:

> Every person should be able to complete—or deliberately leave—each CreatorWorks experience knowing what happened, why it happened, how their information was handled, and what they can do next.

From entry through completion, exit, recovery, and return, CreatorWorks should provide:

- An immediate and honest explanation of what the service does.
- Clear choices with no confusing or hidden steps.
- Accurate expectations about the work and its current state.
- A welcoming path for people at different skill levels.
- A clear result, explanation, or recovery path after every meaningful action.
- Respectful responses and visible acknowledgment.
- Privacy, ownership, consent, and control.
- Helpful recovery when something goes wrong.
- A satisfying conclusion with a clear next step.
- No manipulative prompts, surprise fees, disguised promotion, or artificial urgency.

Satisfaction does not mean granting every request, accepting every work, publishing every comment, or avoiding necessary safeguards. When interests conflict, CreatorWorks should decide in this order:

1. Safety and legal responsibility.
2. Honest expectations.
3. Consent and privacy.
4. Fair treatment.
5. Successful task completion.
6. Ease and delight.

No person's satisfaction may justify deceiving, exploiting, or harming another person.

## The CreatorWorks Loop

The experience is a repeatable human loop rather than a one-time upload:

1. **Clarify** — Explain the problem, intended participant, present capability, and current question.
2. **Make** — Prepare enough of the work for another person to experience.
3. **Present** — Create an honest page with the work's state, disclosures, and invitation.
4. **Invite** — Share it with people who may genuinely benefit or provide relevant perspective.
5. **Experience** — Let those people view, try, or use what currently exists.
6. **Respond** — Gather structured feedback connected to the creator's question.
7. **Acknowledge** — Confirm that the response was received and considered.
8. **Decide** — Record what was learned, what will change, or why no change is planned.
9. **Recognize** — With consent, credit contributions that meaningfully helped.
10. **Return** — Notify the participant and invite them to see or experience what comes next.
11. **Support** — After value and trust are proven, allow recommendation, collaboration, funding, subscription, licensing, or purchase under fair terms.

The loop can repeat as the work grows.

## Work States and Eligibility

The work's state must be visible and must not imply quality approval, safety certification, or endorsement by CreatorWorks.

- **Concept** — The problem and proposed value are described, but nothing is yet testable. Concept sharing should remain private or invitation-only and is not part of the first public collection.
- **Prototype** — Something can be viewed or tested. Feedback focuses on understanding, behavior, and usefulness.
- **Early access** — The work is usable but changing. Known limitations, pricing, access requirements, and expectations must be explicit.
- **Released** — The creator considers the work ready for its stated audience and purpose. “Released” does not mean CreatorWorks has independently verified every claim.

The founding beta should accept testable prototypes, early-access works, and released works. Public discovery should initially exclude concept-only pages.

## The Project Page

A useful project page should answer:

- What is this?
- Who is it for?
- What problem does it address?
- Why did the creator make it?
- What can someone actually do with it today?
- What state is it in?
- Where can it be tried or viewed?
- What will happen when someone follows the external link?
- What specific feedback would help now?
- What has the creator learned, decided, or changed?
- Who helped shape it and chose to be recognized?
- What is the creator doing next?

The page should be a living record of the relationship between the work, its creator, and the people it serves—not a static advertisement.

## Required Work Disclosures

Before a participant follows an external link, the project page should clearly disclose:

- Whether registration is required.
- Whether the work is free, paid, limited, or invitation-only.
- Whether a download or installation is required.
- Supported devices, operating systems, or browsers when relevant.
- Important information the external work may collect.
- Material use of artificial intelligence when it affects expectations, rights, privacy, or safety.
- Known limitations, unfinished areas, or material risks.
- The creator's expected level and timing of response.
- How to report a harmful, deceptive, broken, or misleading listing.

These disclosures should use plain language. They must not be hidden in promotional copy.

## Meaningful Feedback

A blank comment box is not enough. The creator should choose one main feedback goal and a small number of prompts appropriate to the work's state.

Possible prompts include:

- What were you trying to accomplish?
- What did you try?
- What helped you?
- Where did you become confused or stuck?
- What did you expect to happen?
- Who else might benefit from this?
- Would you like to experience a future update?

Feedback should be easy to complete and should not demand unnecessary work. Participants should know whether their response will be private, public, or eligible for creator-approved publication before submitting it.

The feedback outcome should distinguish:

- **Received** — The response reached the creator.
- **Acknowledged** — The creator considered it.
- **Outcome recorded** — The creator explained what was learned, decided, or changed.
- **Recognized** — A meaningful contribution was credited with the participant's consent.

Acknowledgment does not require agreement, implementation, or public recognition.

Public star ratings, anonymous drive-by reviews, downvotes, and global feedback leaderboards are not part of version one.

## Identity, Privacy, and Contact

- Anyone should be able to browse a public page and follow its product link without creating a CreatorWorks account.
- Creators need a verified identity to publish.
- Participants should use a lightweight verified response process rather than creating a full social profile before giving feedback.
- A participant should choose whether their response and identity remain private, appear under a chosen public name, or may be considered for recognition.
- Creator-participant contact should remain inside clearly bounded replies and notifications in version one.
- Direct messages between strangers should not be introduced in version one.
- Notification, export, deletion, blocking, and reporting choices must be understandable and easy to find.

Before beta release, written terms must clarify ownership of works, permitted display of participant responses, use of feedback for improvement, public credit, and the limited permission CreatorWorks needs to operate the service.

## Recognition and Belonging

“Part of something bigger” must be demonstrated through behavior rather than homepage language alone.

Version one should support:

- A visible acknowledgment from the creator.
- An optional “helped shape this work” credit.
- An update showing what was learned, decided, or changed.
- Permission for a participant to remain private or use a chosen public identity.
- A record of constructive participation that emphasizes contribution quality rather than volume.

Community in version one means a visible pattern of people helping useful work improve. It does not yet mean an open social feed, large discussion network, or expectation that every participant become publicly involved.

## Safety and Service Boundary

CreatorWorks controls the quality of its own presentation, invitation, feedback, recognition, notification, moderation, and recovery experiences. It does not control software hosted elsewhere and cannot guarantee that every external work will be safe, useful, available, or satisfying.

To protect trust:

- Founding works should be reviewed manually before broad public discovery.
- External destinations and material changes to them should be monitored or re-reviewed proportionately to risk.
- Participants should see important disclosures before leaving CreatorWorks.
- CreatorWorks should make reporting and returning from a problematic external experience straightforward.
- Misleading claims, harmful software, impersonation, plagiarism, spam, harassment, and manipulation should be prohibited.
- Restricted or high-risk categories must be defined before public discovery begins.
- A clear removal, appeal, and correction process must exist.
- CreatorWorks should never present a listing as an endorsement unless it has a separate, clearly explained review standard.

## Discovery Without a Popularity Contest

Open discovery has a cold-start problem: an empty collection disappoints visitors, while an uncurated collection invites spam and favors people who already have audiences.

Discovery should therefore follow validation rather than launch with the private beta.

When public discovery begins:

1. Include only active, reviewed, testable works.
2. Organize by human need, intended participant, work state, and type of help requested.
3. Publish the standards used to include, exclude, and feature work.
4. Rotate exposure so new and overlooked work can be seen.
5. Provide a correction or reconsideration path for curation decisions.
6. Keep editorial selection separate from payment and popularity.
7. Label any future promotion clearly and keep it out of organic relevance decisions.

A participant should never arrive at an empty category or be led to believe that popularity equals usefulness.

## How Creators Benefit

“Benefit” is broader than immediate payment. CreatorWorks should support a value ladder:

1. Clarity about the work and its intended value.
2. Evidence that the problem matters.
3. Useful human feedback.
4. Recognition for the work.
5. Relationships with early users.
6. Adoption, referrals, or collaboration.
7. Voluntary support, sponsorship, membership, licensing, or sales.

CreatorWorks should demonstrate the first six levels before building native commerce for the seventh.

## Future Business Model Constitution

The exact business model is not decided, but it must follow these boundaries:

- No advertising model that rewards attention captured over work improved.
- No sale of personal behavior or creator-participant relationships.
- No hidden pay-to-rank discovery.
- No fee that is difficult for creators or buyers to understand.
- No platform claim over a creator's intellectual property beyond the limited permission needed to operate the service.
- No forced exclusivity.
- No monetization of participant feedback without clear knowledge and consent.
- No paid feature that secretly weakens fair access to basic publishing, feedback, or organic discovery.
- Creators must understand what they earn, what CreatorWorks earns, and why.
- Free participation and genuine human connection must remain possible.

Possible future models include a transparent transaction fee, optional creator tools, voluntary platform support, sponsorship, and memberships. None should be implemented until the human value loop works.

Business-model research should still occur during validation. Creator interviews should test who may be willing to pay, for what outcome, and under what terms without building payment features prematurely.

## Phased Product Plan

### Phase 0: Manual validation

Purpose: determine whether the CreatorWorks loop is more valuable than sending someone a product link and a generic survey.

Create and test:

- One clear homepage prototype.
- One realistic project-page prototype.
- One structured feedback experience.
- One acknowledgment and outcome update.
- A manually coordinated experience with a small founding group.

Do not build accounts, automated discovery, payments, or a marketplace during this phase.

Initial learning sample:

- Five creators with testable software.
- At least three relevant participants invited per creator.
- At least five completed loops across at least three different works.

These are learning targets, not growth goals or proof of market size.

Move forward only when people understand the purpose without extensive explanation, creators receive useful learning, participants consider their time worthwhile, and no unresolved trust or safety problem undermines the experience.

### Phase 1: Private founding beta — Version One

Purpose: prove that the complete loop can work repeatedly without intensive manual guidance.

Build only:

- A clear homepage.
- Verified creator identity.
- Guided project-page creation.
- Prototype, early-access, or released state.
- Required work and external-link disclosures.
- Private invitation links.
- One specific feedback request per active round.
- Lightweight verified participant responses.
- Participant privacy and display choices.
- Creator acknowledgment and bounded replies.
- An outcome update describing what was learned, decided, or changed.
- Participant notification of that outcome.
- Basic blocking, reporting, moderation, export, and deletion controls.

Public discovery is not part of the private founding beta.

### Phase 2: Curated public discovery

Purpose: help relevant people find useful emerging work without creating a popularity contest.

Begin only when:

- The complete loop works repeatedly without staff explanation.
- Enough active, reviewed works exist to make every visible category useful.
- Curation, correction, moderation, external-link review, and safety processes are ready.
- New creators without existing audiences have a credible path to fair exposure.

Add:

- A small public collection.
- Need-based categories and filters.
- Transparent selection and rotation.
- Space for new and overlooked work.
- Clear participant invitations based on relevance rather than popularity.

### Phase 3: Support and commerce

Purpose: help proven value become sustainable without damaging trust.

Before native payments:

- Test whether creators and participants want external support or purchase links.
- Determine who pays CreatorWorks, for what outcome, and why.
- Select one understandable revenue model.
- Define fees, refunds, taxes, payouts, disputes, fraud controls, and transaction responsibilities.
- Confirm that commerce will not distort feedback, recognition, or organic discovery.

Only then consider sponsorship, membership, licensing, sales, native payments, or a marketplace.

## Version-One Proof

Version one succeeds when:

> A creator presents testable work, invites a relevant person to experience it, receives a thoughtful response, acknowledges that response, and records what was learned, decided, or changed. The participant can see the outcome and knows their time mattered.

This proof does not require public discovery, payments, marketplace features, a social feed, or every participant suggestion becoming a product change.

## Explicitly Not in Version One

- Public discovery.
- Concept-only public listings.
- Payments, payouts, subscriptions, or a marketplace.
- External product hosting, downloads, or deployment.
- A built-in software generator.
- An algorithmic social feed.
- Follower counts, public popularity rankings, or star ratings.
- Direct messages between strangers.
- Complex team collaboration.
- Advertising or sponsored rankings.
- Automated or artificial engagement.
- A large public directory.

## Homepage Message Hierarchy

The version-one homepage should answer four questions in order:

1. **Why should I care?** My software can become more useful through the experience of real people.
2. **What happens here?** I present testable work, invite relevant people, receive thoughtful responses, and show what I learned or decided.
3. **Why is this different?** CreatorWorks closes the loop between feedback and what happens next instead of rewarding empty engagement.
4. **What can I do now?** Present a testable work or respond to a private invitation.

Suggested headline:

> **Make software worth sharing.**

Suggested explanation:

> Present what you are building, invite the people it may help, and learn what to improve through thoughtful human feedback.

Suggested primary action:

- **Present your work**

Suggested invitation action:

- **Respond to an invitation**

“Discover emerging work” should become a public action only in Phase 2, when a useful collection genuinely exists. Selling should be described as part of the longer journey, not as a currently available feature.

## Primary Measure: Completed Loops

The primary measure is the number and quality of completed CreatorWorks loops:

1. A relevant participant meaningfully experiences the work.
2. The participant gives a structured response.
3. The creator acknowledges it.
4. The creator records a learning, decision, or change.
5. The participant is notified and can see the outcome.

A completed loop is evidence of value. A page view, registration, like, or comment alone is not.

## Supporting Success and Guardrail Measures

Measure:

- Time until a work receives its first relevant response.
- The proportion of active works that complete the loop.
- Whether creators consider the responses useful and can explain why.
- Whether participants feel their time was worthwhile.
- Whether both sides found the experience clear, fair, respectful, and complete.
- Whether creators record decisions even when they choose not to make a change.
- Whether participants return to see the outcome or try an update.
- Whether people understand CreatorWorks without extensive explanation.
- Where people encounter confusion, abandonment, or failure.
- Whether recovery resolves a problem without damaging trust.
- Reporting, moderation, privacy, and external-link safety incidents.
- The amount of effort required from CreatorWorks staff to complete each loop.
- In Phase 2, whether creators without existing audiences receive relevant attention.

Do not optimize mainly for registrations, page views, likes, follower counts, time spent, comment volume, or gross transaction value.

## Validation and Release Gates

Do not move to the next phase merely because the current phase has been built.

### Before the private founding beta

- The purpose is understood without extensive explanation.
- The manual loop produces useful learning for creators.
- Participants consider the experience worth their time.
- The acknowledgment and outcome step feels meaningfully better than a generic survey.
- Critical trust, ownership, consent, and safety questions have workable answers.

### Before public discovery

- The loop works repeatedly without intensive staff guidance.
- Public-project eligibility and curation standards are published.
- External-link review and incident response are operational.
- Moderation, correction, removal, and appeal processes exist.
- Every visible category contains enough active, reviewed work to be useful.

### Before native commerce

- The human value loop remains healthy over time.
- Creators demonstrate a real willingness to pay for a defined outcome or accept a transparent transaction model.
- Participants understand the difference between organic relevance, editorial selection, and promotion.
- Payment, tax, refund, dispute, fraud, data, and payout responsibilities are understood.
- Commerce does not reduce trust, feedback quality, fair discovery, or free participation.

## Lessons Adapted From Existing Models

- **GitHub:** Project-centered conversation works because discussion stays attached to the work and its future. CreatorWorks should preserve that context without requiring participants to understand code.
- **Figma Community:** Published resources can evolve and multiple contributors can be credited. CreatorWorks should make work understandable, updateable, and properly attributed.
- **itch.io:** Creator control, flexible release states, limited testing, non-ranked community events, and transparent economics show that distribution can support independence without forcing every work into the same competitive model.
- **Patreon:** A free relationship and direct connection can exist before paid support. CreatorWorks should let belonging and trust form before asking for money.
- **Kickstarter:** People can participate in bringing a project to life rather than acting only as customers, but expectations and project state must be explicit.
- **Gumroad:** Simple selling and discovery can help creators, but marketplace discovery introduces distinct incentives and costs. CreatorWorks should separate organic relevance, editorial selection, paid promotion, and transaction fees if commerce is added.
